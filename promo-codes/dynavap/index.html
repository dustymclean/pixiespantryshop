What DynaVap actually makes

DynaVap does one narrow thing: torch-heated, battery-free dry herb vaporizers, machined in Wisconsin. There is no circuit board, no lithium cell and no firmware. You heat the metal tip with a butane torch or induction heater, and a temperature-indicating cap clicks audibly when the tip reaches extraction temperature — that click is the entire user interface.

The catalogue splits roughly into three things worth knowing before you spend a code on it:

  • Vaporizer tips (the “M” and its machined siblings)
  • Stems, bodies and condensers
  • Induction heaters, caps, o-ring kits and spare parts

That last category is the reason the discount is worth more than it looks. Because every part is serviceable and sold separately, a 10% code applies just as well to a $6 o-ring rebuild kit or a replacement cap as it does to a complete device. If you already own one, this is a restock code as much as a purchase code.

Who this is and is not for

Worth it if: you are tired of replacing a battery device every eighteen months, you want something field-serviceable with a screwdriver, or you want a vaporizer that will still work in ten years because there is nothing in it to die.

Skip it if: you want push-button operation, you do not want to carry a torch, or you want to load it and walk away. It is deliberately slow and manual. That is the design, not a flaw, but it is genuinely not for everyone and I would rather you not spend the money than buy the wrong category of device with my code on it.

Budget for the torch if you do not own one. A single-flame butane torch is the usual starting point; an induction heater is the upgrade that removes the flame entirely.
